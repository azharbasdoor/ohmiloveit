There are moments when our ego grows larger than the circumstances—when the desire to be celebrated blinds us to the pain others are enduring. This letter is an admission; it is about a flower forced to bloom in the wrong winter, and about learning to love by offering space, rather than simply demanding attention.

I have only just realized that perhaps I was too busy chasing spring while you were weathering a harsh winter. I longed for colorful petals to celebrate my day, failing to see that you were struggling just to catch your breath between the rigid, white walls of a hospital room.

Forgive me if my insistence on having flowers on the twelfth of January became an added burden upon your hospital bed. I was so caught up in my own desire to be celebrated that I forgot you were the one who, at that moment, needed strength the most. I regret that my ill-timed request only exhausted you—to the point where you felt the need to pull away and ask for space.

Honestly, it saddens me that we have grown so distant. It hurts that my own ego has kept us from holding onto one another while you are at your weakest. It turns out that, at times, I feel as though your world isn't entirely mine—perhaps not because you're unwilling to share it, but because I haven't yet learned how to enter it the right way; in a way that brings peace rather than distress.

I will give you the space you've asked for. I won't ask for anything more regarding my birthday—it doesn't matter anymore. All that matters is your recovery and your return to health; seeing you well is a far greater celebration than any gift or bouquet of flowers could ever be.

I may not be in that room with you right now, but I hope my prayers slip through the gaps in the window, watching over you even better than my own presence could. Get well soon. I am here, waiting patiently, without causing any disturbance.

In the end, I've learned that loving someone doesn't mean possessing their entire world at every single moment. Sometimes, love is simply about being a source of calm, quiet comfort. I'll tuck away all this longing beneath my pillow, waiting for the time when you no longer have to fight just to catch your breath—so that, together, we might begin a brand-new day.
