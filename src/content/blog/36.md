Have you ever noticed the three-button configuration on a men's suit jacket?

In the world of tailoring, there is a well-known, classic, unwritten rule: "Sometimes, Always, Never." The top button may be fastened occasionally; the middle button must always be fastened when standing; and the bottom button should never be fastened. It is left undone so that the suit's cut drapes perfectly—without restriction—allowing the body the freedom to move.

Yet, how many men truly understand the reason behind that bottom button's existence? Some leave it undone simply out of habit, while others may not even realize it is there at all. It is sewn on with thread just as strong and cut from the same fabric with equal precision, yet it is destined to never be fastened through its buttonhole.

Observing this small detail invites a deeper reflection on space, existence, and how we position ourselves within our relationships.

## Being the Unfastened Button

To imagine what it is like to be that bottom button is to envision a form of quiet devotion.  
It exists not because it lacks a function, but because its very purpose lies in its willingness to refrain from dominating. It holds itself back so that the overall appearance of the suit remains elegant and balanced.

In the dynamics of relationships, women often find themselves in the position of that bottom button—diligently tending to subtle details behind the scenes, providing breathing room when things become stifling, or serving as an invisible boundary that keeps a relationship from becoming rigid or fragile.

A great deal of care, compromise, and energy is woven in—much like the threads that secure a button to a piece of fabric. Yet, it is not uncommon for these subtle efforts to be dismissed as mere decoration or an incidental detail. Often, one enjoys the finished form of a relationship without ever truly grasping the compromises at work that keep that connection comfortable and sustainable.

## Between Existence and Understanding

There is a vast difference between possessing something and understanding it.

A man might wear an impeccably tailored suit, yet—lacking an understanding of how it is constructed—he may feel restricted when he sits down, or worse, end up ruining the very shape of the garment.

The same applies to relationships. One might feel they "have" a partner, yet fail to comprehend that person's fundamental needs, personal boundaries, or the quiet language they express without a word. A perceptive person knows when to tighten a commitment to provide certainty, and when to loosen it to allow for breathing room. Such a person does not merely exist within the relationship; they also cherish how every element within it serves to support the others.

## Beyond What We Take for Granted

For me, paying attention to the buttons on a suit has evolved into more than just a matter of dress—it has become a way of interpreting the reality around me.

I often ask myself and the world around me: do we truly see and understand the silent roles played by those in our lives? Or do we simply enjoy what appears on the surface, never caring to consider the reasons behind their presence?

Because, as I see it, there is nothing more exhausting than being the one who is always there—ever-ready and ever-watchful—only to have one's true value remain entirely misunderstood.
